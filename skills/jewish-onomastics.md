# Jewish onomastics of Congress Poland — name knowledge the models half-know
For gold-corpus cross-checks (P1), extraction normalization (P2/P3), and the variant bridge (P4).

## The layered name reality
One person routinely carries: a Hebrew/religious name (Yitzhak), a Yiddish everyday form
(Itche/Itzik), the Russified register form (Ицекъ/Icek), a Polonized spelling (Icek/Izaak),
and sometimes a later Westernized form (Isaac/Isidore). The register shows the Russified/
Polonized layer. NORMALIZE for value, PRESERVE the page form, NEVER merge two people because
names "mean the same" — equivalence is evidence for matching, not proof of identity.

## Double names
«Icek Moszek», «Sura Dwojra», «Abram Moszek» — ONE person with a compound given name, standard
practice. Do not split into two people; do match on either element when searching.

## Women's surnames
- «урожденной X» / «z domu X» = née X — the maiden name, first-class field.
- Married women appear under husband's surname everywhere except their own birth/marriage acts.
  A negative search on a maiden name proves nothing about a married woman (the source
  project's Sarita lesson).
- Occasionally acts write a married woman with her maiden surname (esp. deaths) — capture as
  written, note the convention.

## Naming customs (soft evidence — never assert as fact)
Ashkenazi custom: children named after DECEASED relatives, typically grandparents; a living
parent's name reused signals the namesake had died (the source project dated a death from
exactly this). A grandson Meir under a grandfather Majer is a pattern worth flagging as
INFERRED — never as a conclusion.

## Town-variant seeds (P4 bridge; from the source project's logged real-world manglings)

Pułtusk = Pultusk = POUFTOUSK = RULTUSK = PULSTUK (all three manglings attested in USHMM
